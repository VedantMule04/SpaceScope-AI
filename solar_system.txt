
============================================================ 1. THE
SOLAR SYSTEM
============================================================

What is the Solar System? The Solar System is the Sun and all the
objects held by the Sun’s gravity. These include eight planets, their
moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, comets, and smaller rocky or icy
objects. The Solar System formed about 4.6 billion years ago from a
cloud of gas and dust.

What is the Sun? The Sun is a star at the centre of the Solar System. It
is made mostly of hydrogen and helium. Its gravity keeps planets and
other objects in orbit. Energy is produced in its core by nuclear
fusion, where hydrogen nuclei combine to form helium and release energy.
Sunlight takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth.

What are the eight planets? The eight planets in order from the Sun are
Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. -
Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. It has a
heavily cratered surface and almost no substantial atmosphere. - Venus
is the hottest planet on average because its thick carbon-dioxide
atmosphere traps heat through an extreme greenhouse effect. - Earth is
the only world currently known to support life. It has liquid water on
much of its surface and a nitrogen-oxygen atmosphere. - Mars is called
the Red Planet because iron minerals in its soil have oxidised, or
rusted. It has the largest volcano known in the Solar System, Olympus
Mons. - Jupiter is the largest planet. It is a gas giant with a powerful
magnetic field and the Great Red Spot, a long-lasting storm. - Saturn is
a gas giant famous for its bright rings, which are made mostly of pieces
of water ice with some rocky material. - Uranus is an ice giant. It
rotates on its side relative to its orbit, giving it extreme seasons. -
Neptune is the most distant planet from the Sun. It is an ice giant with
very fast winds.

Why is Pluto not one of the eight planets? Pluto is classified as a
dwarf planet. It orbits the Sun and is nearly round, but it has not
cleared the neighbourhood around its orbit of other objects. The
International Astronomical Union adopted this planet definition in 2006.

What is the difference between inner and outer planets? Mercury, Venus,
Earth, and Mars are the inner, rocky planets. Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus,
and Neptune are the outer giant planets. The asteroid belt lies between
Mars and Jupiter.

What is a moon or natural satellite? A natural satellite is an object
that orbits a planet or another larger body. Earth’s Moon is a natural
satellite. Some planets have many moons; Mercury and Venus have none.

What are asteroids? Asteroids are small rocky or metallic bodies that
orbit the Sun. Many are found in the asteroid belt between Mars and
Jupiter. They are leftovers from the early Solar System.

What are comets? Comets are icy bodies that orbit the Sun. When a comet
approaches the Sun, heat causes some ice to turn directly into gas.
Escaping gas and dust can form a glowing coma and tails. A comet’s tail
points generally away from the Sun because of sunlight and the solar
wind.

What are meteoroids, meteors, and meteorites? A meteoroid is a small
rocky or metallic object in space. A meteor is the streak of light
produced when it enters an atmosphere and heats the surrounding gas. A
meteorite is a piece that survives the journey and reaches the ground.

What is the asteroid belt? The asteroid belt is a region between Mars
and Jupiter containing many rocky objects orbiting the Sun. It is not a
solid wall; objects are separated by large distances.

What is the Kuiper Belt? The Kuiper Belt is a distant region beyond
Neptune containing many icy bodies, including Pluto. It is one source of
short-period comets.

What is the Oort Cloud? The Oort Cloud is a hypothesised enormous,
distant, roughly spherical reservoir of icy objects surrounding the
Solar System. It has not been directly observed as a complete structure.

What keeps planets in orbit? The Sun’s gravity attracts planets, while
each planet’s forward motion carries it along its path. Together these
produce an orbit rather than a straight-line path into the Sun.

============================================================ 2. GALAXIES
============================================================

What is a galaxy? A galaxy is a huge system of stars, gas, dust, stellar
remnants, and dark matter held together by gravity. Galaxies can contain
millions to trillions of stars.

Which galaxy contains our Solar System? Our Solar System is inside the
Milky Way galaxy. The Solar System is not a galaxy; it is a small system
within the Milky Way.

What type of galaxy is the Milky Way? The Milky Way is a barred spiral
galaxy. It has a central bar-shaped region, a surrounding bulge, and
spiral arms containing stars, gas, and dust.

How many stars are in the Milky Way? Estimates suggest the Milky Way
contains roughly 100 billion to 400 billion stars. The exact number is
uncertain because many stars are faint or difficult to observe.

How many galaxies are in the universe? Astronomers estimate that the
observable universe contains hundreds of billions to perhaps around two
trillion galaxies, depending on methods and assumptions. The exact
number is not known. This estimate refers to the observable universe,
not necessarily the entire universe.

What are the main types of galaxies? - Spiral galaxies have a flattened
disk and spiral arms. The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy. -
Elliptical galaxies are rounded or oval and usually contain older stars
with less cool gas and dust. - Lenticular galaxies have a disk and
central bulge but lack prominent spiral arms. - Irregular galaxies do
not have a clear spiral or elliptical shape.

What is the difference between a galaxy and a solar system? A solar
system is a star and the objects orbiting it. A galaxy contains many
stars and their planetary systems, along with gas, dust, and dark
matter. Our Solar System is one of many star systems in the Milky Way.

What is the nearest large galaxy to the Milky Way? The Andromeda Galaxy
is the nearest large spiral galaxy to the Milky Way. It is about 2.5
million light-years away. The Milky Way and Andromeda are moving toward
each other and are expected to interact in several billion years.

What is a light-year? A light-year is a unit of distance, not time. It
is the distance light travels in one year in a vacuum: about 9.46
trillion kilometres.

============================================================ 3. THE
UNIVERSE ============================================================

What is the universe? The universe includes all space, time, matter, and
energy, as well as the physical laws that describe them. It contains
galaxies, stars, planets, radiation, and other forms of matter and
energy.

What is the Big Bang theory? The Big Bang theory is the leading
scientific explanation for the early evolution of the universe. It says
the universe expanded from an extremely hot, dense early state about
13.8 billion years ago. It was not an explosion into pre-existing empty
space; space itself has expanded.

What evidence supports the Big Bang? Important evidence includes: 1. The
expansion of the universe, observed as distant galaxies generally moving
away from us. 2. The cosmic microwave background, faint radiation left
over from the early hot universe. 3. The observed amounts of light
elements such as hydrogen and helium, which agree with predictions of
early-universe nuclear processes.

What is the observable universe? The observable universe is the part of
the universe from which light has had time to reach us since the early
universe. Its present-day radius is about 46.5 billion light-years. This
is larger than 13.8 billion light-years because space expanded while the
light was travelling.

Is the entire universe the same size as the observable universe? No. The
observable universe is only the region we can, in principle, observe.
The entire universe may be much larger and could even be infinite; its
total size is not known.

Why is the universe expanding? Observations show that space is expanding
on very large scales. In the standard cosmological model, dark energy is
associated with the observed acceleration of this expansion. The exact
nature of dark energy remains unknown.

What is the cosmic microwave background? The cosmic microwave
background, or CMB, is faint microwave radiation coming from all
directions. It was released when the young universe cooled enough for
light to travel freely, about 380,000 years after the Big Bang.

What is dark matter? Dark matter is a form of matter that does not emit,
absorb, or reflect light in the usual way. Scientists infer its presence
from its gravitational effects on galaxies, galaxy clusters, and the
large-scale structure of the universe. Its exact nature is unknown.

What is dark energy? Dark energy is the name given to whatever is
causing the expansion of the universe to accelerate. It is a major part
of the current cosmological model, but its physical nature is not yet
understood.

============================================================ 4. STARS
============================================================

What is a star? A star is a huge, hot ball of gas, mostly hydrogen and
helium, held together by gravity. Most stars produce energy through
nuclear fusion in their cores.

How are stars formed? Stars form inside cold, dense clouds of gas and
dust called molecular clouds. Gravity causes parts of a cloud to
collapse. The centre becomes hotter and denser, forming a protostar.
When the core becomes hot enough for sustained hydrogen fusion, a star
is born.

What is nuclear fusion? Nuclear fusion is a process in which light
atomic nuclei combine to form heavier nuclei. In the Sun, hydrogen
nuclei ultimately combine to form helium, releasing energy.

What is the life cycle of a star? A star’s life depends mainly on its
mass. - A star forms from a collapsing cloud and becomes a protostar. -
It spends most of its life in the main-sequence stage, fusing hydrogen
into helium in its core. - When core hydrogen is used up, the star
changes. A Sun-like star expands into a red giant. - A Sun-like star
sheds its outer layers, creating a planetary nebula, and its core
remains as a white dwarf. - A much more massive star can become a
supergiant and may end in a core-collapse supernova, leaving a neutron
star or black hole.

What is a red giant? A red giant is a late stage in the life of a low-
or medium-mass star. Its outer layers expand and cool after the core’s
hydrogen fuel is depleted.

What is a white dwarf? A white dwarf is the hot, dense leftover core of
a low- or medium-mass star after it has shed its outer layers. It no
longer produces energy through normal core fusion and slowly cools over
time.

What is a supernova? A supernova is an extremely powerful stellar
explosion. Some occur when a massive star’s core collapses; others occur
when a white dwarf in a binary system undergoes a runaway thermonuclear
explosion. Supernovas spread elements into space and can leave behind
neutron stars or black holes.

Do all stars become black holes? No. Only the most massive stellar cores
are expected to collapse into black holes. Lower-mass stars like the Sun
are expected to end as white dwarfs.

What is a neutron star? A neutron star is an extremely dense remnant
left after the core collapse of certain massive stars. A teaspoon of
neutron-star material would have an enormous mass under Earth’s gravity,
although such material cannot be handled like an ordinary substance.

What is a constellation? A constellation is a recognised pattern or
region of the sky named by people. Stars in a constellation may look
close together from Earth but can be at very different distances.

============================================================ 5. BLACK
HOLES ============================================================

What is a black hole? A black hole is a region of space where gravity is
so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape from inside its event
horizon. Black holes are described by Einstein’s theory of general
relativity.

How are black holes formed? Many black holes form when the core of a
very massive star collapses after the star runs out of fuel. Other black
holes, called supermassive black holes, are found in the centres of many
galaxies and can contain millions or billions of times the Sun’s mass.
Their growth history is still being studied.

What is the event horizon? The event horizon is the boundary around a
black hole beyond which escape is impossible. It is not a solid surface.
Once something crosses it, it cannot send a signal back outside.

Can we see a black hole? A black hole itself does not emit light that
can escape from within its event horizon. Scientists detect black holes
by observing their effects on nearby stars and gas, the radiation from
hot material outside the event horizon, gravitational waves, and images
of the shadow region.

What happens near a black hole? Strong gravity can stretch and distort
space and time. Gas falling toward a black hole can form a hot accretion
disk and emit intense radiation. Very close to some black holes,
differences in gravity across an object can stretch it, an effect often
called spaghettification.

Do black holes suck in everything? No. A black hole’s gravity acts like
the gravity of any other object with the same mass when viewed from far
away. If the Sun were replaced by a black hole of the same mass, Earth
would continue in roughly the same orbit, although Earth would lose
sunlight and become cold.

What is a supermassive black hole? A supermassive black hole is a black
hole with millions to billions of solar masses. One is located at the
centre of the Milky Way; it is called Sagittarius A*.

What is the difference between a black hole and a neutron star? Both can
be remnants of massive stars and have very strong gravity. A neutron
star has a physical surface made of extremely dense matter. A black hole
has an event horizon, beyond which no light or matter can escape.

============================================================ 6. EARTH
AND THE MOON
============================================================

What is Earth’s rotation? Rotation is Earth’s spin around its axis. One
rotation relative to the Sun takes about 24 hours and causes the cycle
of day and night.

What is Earth’s revolution? Revolution is Earth’s motion around the Sun.
One orbit takes about 365.25 days. The extra quarter-day is why leap
years are used to keep the calendar aligned with Earth’s orbit.

What causes seasons? Seasons are caused mainly by Earth’s axial tilt of
about 23.5 degrees, not by changes in Earth’s distance from the Sun.
When a hemisphere tilts toward the Sun, it receives more direct sunlight
and has longer days, producing summer. The opposite hemisphere
experiences winter.

What is the Moon? The Moon is Earth’s natural satellite. It orbits Earth
and reflects sunlight; it does not make its own visible light.

Why does the Moon have phases? The Moon’s phases occur because we see
different portions of its sunlit half as it orbits Earth. The main
phases include new moon, first quarter, full moon, and third quarter.

Why do we see nearly the same side of the Moon? The Moon rotates once on
its axis in about the same time it takes to orbit Earth. This is called
synchronous rotation or tidal locking, so nearly the same hemisphere
faces Earth.

What is a solar eclipse? A solar eclipse occurs when the Moon passes
between Earth and the Sun and blocks some or all of the Sun from a
location on Earth. A total solar eclipse occurs when the Sun is
completely covered from the observer’s location.

What is a lunar eclipse? A lunar eclipse occurs when Earth passes
between the Sun and the Moon, and Earth’s shadow falls on the Moon. A
total lunar eclipse can make the Moon appear reddish because Earth’s
atmosphere filters and bends sunlight.

Why don’t eclipses happen every month? The Moon’s orbit is tilted by
about five degrees relative to Earth’s orbital plane. Most months, the
Sun, Earth, and Moon do not line up closely enough for an eclipse.

What causes ocean tides? Tides are caused mainly by the Moon’s
gravitational pull, with the Sun also contributing. Earth’s rotation and
the changing positions of the Moon and Sun produce patterns of high and
low tides.

What is the difference between a rotation and a revolution? Rotation is
an object’s spin around its own axis. Revolution is an object’s orbit
around another object. Earth’s rotation causes day and night; Earth’s
revolution around the Sun, together with its axial tilt, defines the
year and seasons.

============================================================ 7. SPACE
EXPLORATION: NASA, ISRO, AND MISSIONS
============================================================

What is space exploration? Space exploration is the use of telescopes,
satellites, robotic spacecraft, landers, rovers, and human missions to
study space and celestial objects. It helps scientists understand Earth,
the Solar System, and the universe.

What is NASA? NASA stands for the National Aeronautics and Space
Administration. It is the United States government agency responsible
for the country’s civilian space programme and much of its aeronautics
research. NASA conducts science missions, develops space technology, and
works with international partners.

What is ISRO? ISRO stands for the Indian Space Research Organisation. It
is India’s national space agency. ISRO develops launch vehicles,
satellites, and spacecraft for science, communication, navigation, Earth
observation, and exploration.

What is Chandrayaan-1? Chandrayaan-1 was India’s first lunar mission,
launched in 2008. It studied the Moon from orbit and helped provide
important evidence for water-related molecules on the lunar surface.

What is Chandrayaan-2? Chandrayaan-2 was launched in 2019. Its orbiter
continues to study the Moon. The lander attempt did not achieve a
successful soft landing, but the orbiter has returned valuable
scientific data.

What is Chandrayaan-3? Chandrayaan-3 successfully achieved a soft
landing near the Moon’s south polar region on 23 August 2023. India
became the fourth country to achieve a soft lunar landing. The lander
was named Vikram and the rover was named Pragyan.

What was the purpose of Chandrayaan-3? Its goals included demonstrating
safe landing and rover operations and conducting surface experiments.
The lander and rover studied local lunar soil and surface conditions
during their mission.

What is the Mars Orbiter Mission? India’s Mars Orbiter Mission, also
called Mangalyaan, was launched in 2013 and entered Mars orbit in 2014.
It demonstrated India’s ability to reach Mars orbit and studied the
planet using scientific instruments.

What is NASA’s Perseverance rover? Perseverance is a NASA rover that
landed on Mars in February 2021. It studies the planet’s geology and
climate, searches for signs of ancient microbial life, and collects
samples for possible future return to Earth.

What is the James Webb Space Telescope? The James Webb Space Telescope
is a space observatory that mainly observes infrared light. It studies
the early universe, galaxies, stars, planetary systems, and the
atmospheres of exoplanets. It is a partnership involving NASA, ESA, and
the Canadian Space Agency.

What is the International Space Station? The International Space
Station, or ISS, is a large crewed research laboratory orbiting Earth.
Astronauts from different countries live and work there, conducting
experiments in microgravity and testing technologies for future
exploration.

What is a rover? A rover is a robotic vehicle designed to travel across
the surface of another world. Rovers carry cameras and scientific
instruments to study rocks, soil, weather, and the environment.

What is a space probe? A space probe is an uncrewed spacecraft sent to
study a planet, moon, asteroid, comet, or region of space. Probes can
fly past, orbit, land, or travel through space to collect data.

============================================================ 8.
ASTRONOMY BASICS
============================================================

What is astronomy? Astronomy is the scientific study of objects and
phenomena beyond Earth’s atmosphere, including planets, stars, galaxies,
and the universe.

What is the difference between astronomy and astrology? Astronomy is a
natural science based on observation, mathematics, and physical laws.
Astrology is a belief system that claims celestial positions influence
human affairs; its predictions are not supported by scientific evidence.

What is a telescope? A telescope collects and focuses light or other
electromagnetic radiation so distant objects can be studied. Optical
telescopes observe visible light. Radio telescopes detect radio waves.
Space telescopes observe from above much or all of Earth’s atmosphere.

What is a satellite? A satellite is an object that orbits another
object. Natural satellites include moons. Artificial satellites are
human-made spacecraft placed in orbit for communication, navigation,
weather forecasting, Earth observation, and science.

What is an artificial satellite used for? Artificial satellites support
GPS and navigation, television and internet communication, weather
forecasting, disaster monitoring, mapping, climate research, and
scientific observations.

What is gravity? Gravity is the attraction associated with mass and
energy. It keeps planets in orbit around stars, moons in orbit around
planets, and holds galaxies together. In Einstein’s general relativity,
gravity is described as the curvature of spacetime.

What is a light-year? A light-year is a distance, not a duration. It is
about 9.46 trillion kilometres, the distance light travels in one year
through a vacuum.

What is an astronomical unit? An astronomical unit, or AU, is
approximately the average distance between Earth and the Sun: about
149.6 million kilometres. It is commonly used for distances within the
Solar System.

What is an orbit? An orbit is the path one object follows around another
under gravity. Orbits can be circular or elliptical. Most planetary
orbits are slightly elliptical.

What is an exoplanet? An exoplanet is a planet that orbits a star
outside our Solar System. Astronomers have confirmed thousands of
exoplanets using methods such as detecting a planet’s transit across its
star or measuring the star’s motion caused by the planet’s gravity.

What is the difference between a meteor and a meteorite? A meteor is the
visible streak of light in an atmosphere. A meteorite is the surviving
piece of the object that reaches the ground.

What is the solar wind? The solar wind is a continuous flow of charged
particles released by the Sun’s outer atmosphere. It interacts with
planetary magnetic fields and can produce auroras.

What are auroras? Auroras are colourful lights in the sky, usually near
polar regions. They occur when charged particles from space interact
with gases in a planet’s upper atmosphere, guided by its magnetic field.

What is the electromagnetic spectrum? The electromagnetic spectrum
includes radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet,
X-rays, and gamma rays. Astronomers observe different parts of this
spectrum to learn about objects and processes in space.

============================================================ 9. EXTRA
SPACE SCIENCE TOPICS
============================================================

What is an exoplanet atmosphere? An exoplanet atmosphere is the layer of
gases surrounding a planet outside our Solar System. Scientists can
sometimes study it when starlight passes through the atmosphere during a
transit.

What is a nebula? A nebula is a large cloud of gas and dust in space.
Some nebulae are regions where stars form; others are material expelled
by dying stars.

What is a star cluster? A star cluster is a group of stars held together
by gravity or sharing a common origin. Open clusters are loosely bound
groups, while globular clusters are dense, roughly spherical collections
of old stars.

What is a binary star? A binary star system contains two stars that
orbit a common centre of mass. Many stars exist in multiple-star
systems.

What is a pulsar? A pulsar is a rapidly rotating neutron star that emits
beams of radiation. If a beam sweeps across Earth, telescopes can detect
regular pulses.

What is a quasar? A quasar is an extremely bright active galactic
nucleus powered by material falling toward a supermassive black hole. It
can outshine the combined light of many stars in its host galaxy.

What is a galaxy cluster? A galaxy cluster is a large group of galaxies
bound together by gravity. Clusters contain galaxies, hot gas, and dark
matter.

What is a gravitational wave? A gravitational wave is a ripple in
spacetime produced by accelerating massive objects, such as two black
holes orbiting and merging. Gravitational waves were directly detected
for the first time in 2015.

What is a habitable zone? The habitable zone around a star is the range
of distances where a planet could, under suitable atmospheric
conditions, have liquid water on its surface. Being in the habitable
zone does not prove that a planet has life.

Is there life beyond Earth? No life beyond Earth has been confirmed.
Scientists search for signs of life by studying Mars, icy moons such as
Europa and Enceladus, and planets orbiting other stars.

What is microgravity? Microgravity is a condition in which objects
appear nearly weightless, such as aboard the International Space
Station. Gravity is still present there; the station and its contents
are in continuous free fall around Earth.

What is re-entry? Re-entry is the return of a spacecraft or object into
a planet’s atmosphere. Air resistance compresses and heats the air
around the object, creating extreme thermal conditions that require heat
shields or other protection.

============================================================ 10. QUICK
FACTS AND COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
============================================================

-   There are eight officially recognised planets in our Solar System.
-   The Sun is a star, not a planet.
-   The Milky Way is the galaxy that contains our Solar System.
-   Our Solar System is not the same thing as the Milky Way.
-   The Moon shines by reflecting sunlight.
-   Seasons are caused mainly by Earth’s axial tilt, not Earth’s
    changing distance from the Sun.
-   The far side of the Moon is not permanently dark; it receives
    sunlight during the lunar day.
-   Space is not completely empty; it contains particles, radiation,
    magnetic fields, and matter.
-   Sound cannot travel through the near-vacuum of outer space in the
    usual way because it needs a material medium.
-   A light-year measures distance, not time.
-   Black holes do not automatically pull in everything in the universe.
-   The Big Bang describes the expansion of space from a hot, dense
    early state; it was not an ordinary explosion at one point in empty
    space.
-   The observable universe is not necessarily the whole universe.
-   No confirmed evidence of life beyond Earth has been found so far.
-   Pluto is a dwarf planet, not one of the eight planets.
